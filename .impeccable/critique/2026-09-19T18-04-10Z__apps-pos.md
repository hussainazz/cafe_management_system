## Design Specificity Verdict

The POS is recognizably Run Cafe in structure: Persian RTL, physical-table dashboard, touch-scale interactions, Estedad, direct service vocabulary, and a calm operational posture. The dark treatment is not yet equally authored. It feels like the light-mode component recipe, with warm browns substituted for white and a pale teal substituted for the light accent.

The deterministic detector found no source-pattern findings. A live injected overlay found one low-contrast-text warning in navigation/header; visual inspection confirms this is a meaningful signal even though the overlay could not identify a source selector.

## Overall Impression

The warm non-black base is a good instinct. The central problem is not insufficient darkness: it is collapsed depth. Canvas, regions, cards, controls, and overlays occupy nearly the same brown value range, then attempt to compensate with a visible outline around almost everything. This weakens the table-first hierarchy precisely when the POS needs to be fastest to scan.

## What Is Working

- Table-first layout, RTL composition, real café labels, Toman numerals, touch targets, and inline product-option disclosure are product-specific and operationally sound.
- The theme avoids generic pure black and pure white in its foundational canvas/text pair; warm dark canvas plus parchment-like copy can preserve the café identity.
- Reduced-motion handling, selected states, drag feedback, and direct service flow are mature interaction foundations.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Collapsed depth ladder and border lattice

The dark ladder from `#12100d` through `#1d1813`, `#231d17`, `#292019`, and `#2c241d` is too compressed in the rendered dashboard. Table tiles, rails, product bays, cards, cart rows, panels, and controls become nearly the same muddy espresso brown. The repeated `rgba(..., .16)` border around each of them turns the screen into an outlined field of isolated boxes.

This slows scanning, fragments the workspace, and makes normal, dense operational UI feel generic rather than calm. Use a more legible neutral elevation ladder, reserve outlines for controls/data-row separation, and create macro-regions primarily with fill and spacing. Most product items should be quieter than the current order and its primary command.

### [P0] Accent color has drifted from the product and is semantically overloaded

The dark accent family (`#94b4b1` through `#d1e3de`) is powdery/minty. It powers primary actions, selected categories, active tables, money emphasis, focus, and in places positive state. In the live dashboard it cools the otherwise warm café world and makes selection, action, activity, and success hard to distinguish.

Keep the warm, restrained café identity. Reassign a low-chroma cool teal only to a narrow operational meaning such as selected/connected where it earns that role. Make the primary command and focus language a restrained coffee-copper/amber family rather than a light mint fill. Do not make the existing teal more saturated.

### [P1] Text hierarchy is technically legible but not visually decisive

Primary, secondary, and muted copy are all warm and readable, but titles, prices, item names, metadata, and state labels often live inside equal-weight outlined cards. Differentiation is then mostly font weight. `#756b5e` disabled copy becomes too recessive on the warm dark canvas, and small connection/status labels do not lead the eye.

Define display, primary, secondary, quiet, and disabled roles with greater intended separation. Reserve strongest parchment contrast for a table number, active task, monetary total, and one commit action—not for every label. Use text hierarchy before adding more borders or semantic fills.

### [P1] Card, button, elevation, and action grammar are over-applied

Tiles, categories, product cards, order rows, manager cards, dialogs, buttons, and some status panels all use the same rounded rectangle + border + fill formula; several add shadow too. On the order desk this leaves the payment action, printing, transfer, edit, and deletion competing at the same perceptual level. A full state fill, colored outline, and colored dot also repeat one message three times.

Use only two structural elevation levels in routine operation. Make product inventory tiles mostly quiet; let the live order, total, and one commit action have the strong contrast. Separate destructive actions by position, space, and a dedicated destructive grammar rather than treating them as another nearby text action.

### [P1] Dialogs, forms, overlays, and semantic states need a dark-specific grammar

Modal cards share the ordinary panel surface and a common shadow; inputs repeat the panel surface/border recipe; alerts frequently combine semantic border, filled tint, and colored text. The 66% scrim is sound, but the dialog itself needs a true overlay surface. The live login view also shows browser-default light input surfaces against the dark shell, an unmistakable coherence break. Focus styling is malformed/absent in the current CSS output, and whole-control `opacity: .55` is a weak disabled-state model.

Give overlays a dedicated, subtly lighter and cooler/warm-neutral top surface; define input rest, hover, focus, filled, invalid, and disabled states deliberately; use one dominant semantic signal plus an icon/label, not three redundant color treatments.

## Persona Red Flags

**Staff under rush:** The order desk can expose 14 categories, product choices, basket, total, and several actions at once. With every region outlined in near-identical brown, finding the next action takes visual parsing before task execution.

**Manager settling a complex order:** Payment, print, transfer, edit, and deletion controls share too much visual grammar. The theme does not make a commit/payment decision feel clearly distinct from reversible utility actions.

**Low-light operator:** Small muted labels and color dots remain textually available but do not have enough perceptual force. Connection, call, occupancy, and disabled condition should be recognizable before their labels are read.

## Minor Observations

- Preserve the warm near-black canvas; do not move to `#000` or corporate charcoal.
- `--background-subtle` is available but underused as a macro-region level.
- Some shadows remain hard-coded for light-theme values, so their dark behavior is not fully token-governed.
- White text on pale dark-theme accent fills reads as an arbitrary flash; introduce an explicit on-accent token after the palette changes.
- Primary hover currently becomes lighter/paler rather than firmer; model hover and pressed intent independently.
- The open-order panel and total provide the clearest moment of direction. Use that as the seed grammar for the rest of dark mode.

## Recommended Direction

Aim for a **night-service counter**: roasted espresso/wood neutrals, parchment typography, restrained copper command cues, and a quieter cool teal limited to specific operational meaning. The feeling should be warm, reliable, and built for a café under low ambient light—not a generic dark SaaS dashboard.

Keep: warm non-black foundation; Persian RTL and Estedad; table-first architecture; large touch targets; inline option disclosure; direct labels; restrained animation; no marketing gloss.

Reconsider: neutral surface/elevation ladder; `--border-default` and `--border-subtle`; `--accent`, hover, active, on-accent and focus roles; success/warning/danger/info mappings and their soft variants; display/secondary/quiet/disabled text; `--surface-overlay`, shadow, and scrim; input/disabled tokens.

## Highest-Impact Sequence

1. Reset neutral depth, elevation, and border policy across the POS.
2. Reassign accent/brand/semantic color roles so selected, positive, warning, destructive, and primary-command states stop sharing teal.
3. Establish an unmistakable primary-versus-utility-versus-destructive action hierarchy.
4. Rebuild text, disabled, focus, input, dialog, and overlay grammar around those tokens.
5. Reduce visual density in tables, product grids, manager forms, and dense data rows without reducing operational information.

## Questions to Consider

What should a staff member spot first from two metres away: a waiter call, an open balance, or a ready table? The answer should drive the color hierarchy rather than assigning all three a variation of the same accent.

## Design Specificity Verdict

The authenticated POS is clearly authored around Run Cafe operations: Persian RTL, named physical tables, Toman amounts, QR-family routing, waiter calls, and settlement/print flows are product-specific. Its visual language is nevertheless a competent but interchangeable cream-and-teal SaaS card system; character lives primarily in data and copy rather than an explicit operational grammar.

The deterministic scan found zero issues in `apps/pos`. This does not contradict the review: the largest risk is a runtime visual gap, not a static anti-pattern. Browser evidence reached the unauthenticated POS screen only; credentials were not used. Mutation preflight succeeded, but the live overlay server did not start within ten seconds, so no human overlay was shown.

## Overall Impression

The table board and recovery model are calm and operationally literate. The biggest opportunity is to make the important, risky actions explicit and teachable, starting with a deliberately designed re-authentication screen.

## What's Working

- Table states communicate more than color through text, icons, duration, and totals.
- Recovery language names the operational consequence and gives a specific retry action rather than a generic failure.
- The table-first workspace respects the actual service sequence and uses practical touch-target sizing.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Re-authentication screen is outside the design system

`pos-session-boundary.tsx` renders `session-screen`, `session-card`, `sign-in`, `primary-button`, `inline-error`, and `loading-dot`, while `globals.css` has no corresponding selectors. The verified desktop login view falls into a horizontal, default-form flow. This is especially damaging after session expiry, when staff need immediate reassurance and a quick return to work. Add a responsive RTL entry layout: centered bounded card, vertical field/action stack, high-contrast focus/error/loading states, and stable autofill styling.

Suggested command: `$impeccable polish apps/pos`

### [P1] Transfer and QR reassignment are hidden high-consequence gestures

Long press and drag can be efficient for experienced staff, but the permanent UI does not advertise how to invoke them or what shared-QR routing means. A new or interrupted staffer must remember a 600 ms long press before they can safely transfer an order or direct a scan. Add a visible transfer mode and a labelled QR-route action/indicator with an explanatory first-use cue; retain gestures only as accelerators.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden apps/pos`

### [P1] Payment and print compete in a flat action stack

The occupied-table panel makes settlement primary, then places print and other consequential commands in a set of nearly equal secondary actions. Under rush conditions that increases scan time and mis-tap risk. Group actions into “service”, “print”, and “danger” zones; anchor printing progress and success/failure to the action that launched it; keep the last successful outcome visible long enough to confirm completion.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout apps/pos`

### [P2] Order entry asks staff to attend to three equal regions at once

The category rail, broad product grid, and active-order summary all demand attention simultaneously. That is manageable at rest but costly when someone is taking a rapid order while responding to a waiter call. Make the active task phase more explicit: selected-table context, product choice, then review/settlement. Add a fast lane for recent/frequent items or search before expanding catalog density.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill apps/pos`

### [P2] Status distinction is vulnerable to glare and peripheral vision

The small 7 px status dots and modest cream/teal/orange tone shifts are elegant but subtle for a lit café POS. Preserve text labels, then strengthen state by combining a larger state icon/edge treatment with color. Keep the pulse reserved for truly urgent waiter calls and provide an equally visible non-motion state.

Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt apps/pos`

## Persona Red Flags

**Rushed café staff:** Cannot discover long press or safely parse the multiple action choices at a glance. A waiter-call table also changes the normal opening interaction, forcing a separate handling model during interruption.

**New or cover staff:** Has no persistent explanation of “QR مشترک”, routing assignment, or transfer gestures. They may avoid a necessary corrective action rather than risk moving the wrong table’s order.

**Interrupted manager:** The recovery banner gives a good next step, but session expiry can return them to the visually broken entry surface without enough confidence that their work remains intact.

## Minor Observations

- The manager navigation uses a text gear glyph instead of the established icon system.
- `workspace-header` uses LTR direction around RTL content, which invites alignment exceptions.
- Reduce waiter-call motion is handled, but default pulsing should be carefully bounded to avoid constant ambient stress.

## Questions to Consider

- Can a staff member who has never been trained successfully transfer or reroute a table without being told about a long press?
- Which table-state facts need a persistent board legend instead of being inferred from color and card content?
- Could the desk act like a clear sequence—context, choose, review—instead of three simultaneous work surfaces?
